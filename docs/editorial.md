# Editorial guide

Low Expectations turns familiar encouragement into small, recognisable disappointments. Aim for dry humour about everyday situations. Avoid hostility towards the reader, long explanations, and punchlines that need specialist knowledge.

## Writing and translation

- Use one idea per entry, usually a setup and a short turn. A line break may separate them.
- Vary advice, observations, and miniature stories. Avoid starting every line with “You”.
- Translate the joke into natural German rather than copying English word order. Use Swiss spelling, including ss rather than ß, and familiar Swiss terms where they read naturally.
- Keep both languages within 110 characters. Check the longer translation in an OG quadrant.
- Avoid weekday, holiday, or season claims: entries rotate continuously from an anchor date.
- Do not copy attributed quotations or invent an author. These entries have no author label.

## Repetition review

The build rejects duplicate IDs and text, ignoring differences in punctuation, spacing, and case. It also checks translation presence and length. These checks cannot judge whether two jokes have the same premise or whether a translation is funny; review those by reading the catalogue.

For each addition, compare the setup, subject, and punchline with existing entries. Reusing a broad subject such as shopping is fine; repeating the same turn with different nouns needs a rewrite. Check neighbouring entries for repeated images or phrasing.
